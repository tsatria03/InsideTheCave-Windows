unrelease:
Once the Tutorial's lessons are over, your screen reader says how to skip the practice: press Enter to say Practice skipped and go to the main menu. You can change the key with F1.
The Tutorial now teaches exactly twelve things, a coin, a torch, a monster and bats in each lane, one at a time in a shuffled order, each waiting until the last has passed, been killed or been caught, then three more seconds. After that it tells you to practise, and the cave plays like the real game at a steady speed, with nothing more said.
The Tutorial now names each kind of thing only the first time it appears in each of the cave's three lanes, such as A coin appeared in the left lane, then lets you learn it by its sound, and says when you have met everything. Passed lines now say the lane, such as Coin passed, in the left lane.

26.10.03-2:
Control stops the Windows voice at once, as it stops a screen reader.
A Tutorial on the main menu, after Play, teaches the game at a pace that never speeds up: one thing comes at a time, and a Windows voice says what each one is, where it is and what to do, when it has passed you or you have caught it, and what your thrown torch did. Nothing in it is saved or counted.
Play now says your keys on your first two games only, and the original's short instructions are no longer said; the Tutorial takes their place.
A Stats row on the main menu reads your games played, longest run, fastest speed, coins, time played, torches picked up, and the bats and monsters you frightened, killed and dodged, for each difficulty and all time.
Score on the main menu is now Scores, and each saved score also says its coins and how long you survived.
The result screen is now a list of rows: your score, your coins, how long you survived and the speed you reached, then your name, Replay and Menu.
Play now asks you to choose a difficulty. Easy runs from speed 5.0 to 3.0 with a torch that lasts longer, Medium from 4.0 to 2.0 like the original, and Hard from 3.0 all the way to 1.0 with a torch that burns out sooner. Each has its own best five, and your old best five is now your Easy one. On Medium and Hard your footsteps run from the start.
Press S in a game for your score, C for your coins, and E for how many times the cave has sped up, from 0 to 40. You can change the keys with F1.
Press T in a game to hear how much torch you have left, from Torch full to No torch. You can change the key with F1.
Trying to throw with no torch now says No torch to throw, instead of doing nothing.
The earphone warning is now said by a Windows voice, as the instructions are, instead of your screen reader, and the main menu waits for it to finish. Any key still skips it.

26.10.03-1:
When a bat dodges your torch, its sound goes with it to its new lane, so you hear where it went.
A bat your torch hits now dodges into another lane and stays there. Before, it went straight back into its lane, so throwing at a bat never saved you.
The cave now starts slower, at speed 5.0 instead of 4.0, and speeds up in even steps of 0.1 for longer, to a top speed of 1.0 after nearly seven minutes, much faster than the original's 1.96.
Torches on the path now jingle from their lane as they come, so you can find them by ear, and coins ding instead, with the coin sound from the game's early versions.
A sound plays when your torch burns all the way out.
Your footsteps run with you from your lane: a walk at first, and a run after about two minutes, as the cave speeds up.
The game's music now plays at full volume, much louder than before, and Page Down in a game turns it down.
The main menu, the Score screen and the result screen have music, from the game's early versions. It stops when a game starts, and does not play on the pause menu.
Page Up and Page Down now change the music: in a game the track, saying track volume, and on the menus the menu music, saying menu volume.
Home and End make the whole game louder and quieter while you play, and your screen reader says master volume. On the menus and the pause menu they still go to the first and last row.
The menus wrap around, so Down on the last row goes back to the first, and Up on the first row goes to the last.
Bumping into the cave wall sounds from the side you bumped into, not from the middle.
Each move sounds from the lane you move into, on the left, in the middle or on the right.

26.10.02-1:
A readme beside the game says how to play, and credits.txt says who made the game and lists the licenses.
No two scores in your best five are ever the same. A score that is already there is not saved again.
Pausing opens a menu with Resume, Restart and Quit to menu, and Escape or P still resumes the game.
A blank name still saves your score, as unnamed player. The original threw the score away.
After a game you type your name, and a score that beats your fifth best is saved. Score on the main menu reads your best five.
The game opens on the original's earphone warning, then the main menu, with Play, Score and Quit. Any key skips the warning.
The sound of taking a coin comes from you, not from the middle lane.
A torch can no longer be thrown in the second after you die.
A coin, a torch on the path, or a monster reaching you always counts, however the game notices it touching you. The original could miss some of them.
Monsters keep coming after you kill one with a torch. In the original, a monster killed at the wrong moment could leave the cave silent for good.
No sound is ever louder than full volume, so nothing peaks.
Page Up and Page Down set the volume, and settings.json holds the overall volume and the music volume, from 0 to 100.
F1 lists the keys and lets you change them. By default A or Left Arrow moves left, D or Right Arrow moves right, W or Up Arrow throws your torch, and P pauses.
P or Escape pauses the game and continues it, and leaving the window pauses it too.
On your first three games, a Windows voice says the original's instructions in your language, then your screen reader says your keys, and the first monster comes once both are done.
Your screen reader says "Torch low" when your torch starts to dim.
Every coin jingles as it comes down its lane, so you can find the coins by ear.
Each monster's roar and the bats' sound come from their own lane, left, ahead or right, so you can hear which lane to leave.
Inside The Cave can be played on Windows from its source, with the original game's own sounds.
